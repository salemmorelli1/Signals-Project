Overall conclusion

What the completed evidence supports—and what remains unresolved.

Simulation conclusion

The broader blind joint torus-valued SSM executed coherently across the full factorial simulation, but neither inference architecture demonstrated superior frequency-recovery accuracy. Performance was governed primarily by the physical SNR and channel conditions rather than by the choice of analytical versus amortized proposal score.

Accuracy

No architecture advantage

The surrogate mean frequency MSE was 238.41 Hz² versus 246.76 Hz² for the analytical score. Across 100 independent seed profiles, the paired difference was −8.35 Hz², 95% t CI [−24.94, 8.25], and the architecture effect was not significant, F(1, 99) = 0.78, p = .378.

Physical drivers

Environment dominates

SNR and channel complexity produced strong frequency-MSE effects, with Holm-adjusted p values below 10−6. Their interaction was also significant, pHolm = .000134, confirming that noise severity depends on propagation structure.

Claim boundary

Real-data inference remains

The surrogate recorded 1.58 versus 1.51 ms/sample under fixed analytical-first execution. That timing contrast is descriptive, not causal. WiSig preparation is complete, but locked inference remains required before external-performance or operational claims.
